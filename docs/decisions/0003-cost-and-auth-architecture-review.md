# ADR-0003 — Cost and Authentication Architecture Review

## Status

Accepted on 2026-09-23. Partially supersedes [ADR-0002](0002-application-architecture-and-stack.md) **only for authentication**: Clerk replaces self-hosted Better Auth. It also replaces ADR-0002's always-active Neon estimate as the expected low-traffic MVP estimate; that estimate remains a conservative ceiling. No service has been provisioned or code implemented.

## Context and decision priority

TASK-002 selected Railway web/cron, Neon Launch PostgreSQL, self-hosted Better Auth, pg-boss, private R2 EU, Resend, and local text/OCR. The owner accepts roughly €20–30/month incremental initial infrastructure spend when it buys data safety, security, reliability, or lower solo-developer burden. This review prioritizes data safety/correctness, security, reliability, solo maintainability, operations, cost, portability, then scale. All prices below are official USD list prices checked **2026-09-23**, not EUR quotes or account-specific invoices. See the [revised cost model](../architecture/cost-model.md).

The original ~$26.5/month gross MVP figure comprised ~$6.25 Railway web, ~$19.59 Neon, ~$0.17 worker, and ~$0.47 AI; R2, auth, email, observability, and separate OCR API fees were modeled at $0. Neon was the largest line because 0.25 CU × 730 hours × $0.106/CU-hour = $19.35 compute before storage/history. This was an **always-active upper scenario at the modeled compute size**, not a Neon subscription or a proven effect of pg-boss. The revised worker calculation rounds to ~$0.16 using Railway's published monthly rate, without materially changing that historical total.

## PostgreSQL comparison

Development remains local Docker PostgreSQL at $0 incremental for all three options; provider Free tiers can be used for disposable integration tests but are not the production recovery plan. All three speak PostgreSQL over TLS, work with Drizzle SQL migrations and pg-boss, and support an EU region; the difference is who operates the database and how recovery is billed. Migration between them is a reviewed logical dump/restore plus extension/role/RLS checks, not a zero-effort switch.

**Decision:** Keep Neon Launch. The payment buys a managed database, configurable seven-day recovery, pooling, and less production database maintenance. Railway's new PITR tooling narrows the recovery-feature gap, but does not make its PostgreSQL engine managed. The estimated few-dollar MVP saving from Railway PostgreSQL under the revised activity model does not justify shifting database operations to one developer. Confirm actual Neon CU-hours and restore drills before launch; do not imply a Launch SLA.

## pg-boss and Neon compute

Neon scales an idle compute to zero after five minutes; frequent connections/background queries reset that timer ([Neon compute](https://neon.com/docs/manage/endpoints/)). pg-boss can prevent sleep **if** a long-running web/worker instance runs its supervisors or frequent polling. Its current [constructor options](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/master/docs/api/constructor.md) include `supervise: false`, `schedule: false`, `migrate: false`, and `useListenNotify: false` (the last is already default); `LISTEN/NOTIFY` would need a dedicated session connection and cannot use transaction-pooled PgBouncer. The chosen finite worker uses manual `fetch`/`complete`/`fail` ([job API](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/master/docs/api/jobs.md)) and closes after a bounded batch. Keep recurring maintenance/supervision in a tested finite command, not the always-on web process. Do not silently disable required queue maintenance. Verify this producer/maintenance split against the pinned pg-boss version and observe Neon activity before production.

Even with that split, a ten-minute cron query followed by Neon's five-minute idle timer implies roughly half the month active before job runtime or web traffic. The revised MVP case budgets **60%** active time (438 hours): 0.25 × 438 × $0.106 = ~$11.61 compute, plus ~$0.24 storage/history/snapshots, or **~$11.85 Neon**. The old $19.59 line is the always-active sensitivity. A continuously supervising web instance or heavier traffic can drive cost back toward it. Railway PostgreSQL avoids a Neon scale-to-zero interaction, but runs continuously and adds database operating work. **Keep pg-boss**; a Redis/managed queue is a new service and does not remove the need for PostgreSQL or justify its cost at this volume.

Seven-day fixed session lifetime and one-day application logs on Hobby. Custom UI flows are possible but still need design/test work. [Pricing](https://clerk.com/pricing), [Next.js](https://clerk.com/docs/nextjs/getting-started/quickstart), [custom flow](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/development/custom-flows/authentication/email-password). |
| Engineering and failure ownership | Debiro owns auth tables/migrations, verification/reset email delivery, password/session configuration, multi-instance rate-limit storage, CSRF/origin handling, upgrades/security patches, abuse monitoring, tests and incident debugging. | Data/control and migration | Identity and sessions remain in Frankfurt PostgreSQL; easier export and less identity-provider lock-in. | Clerk stores identity data in the US and offers **no EU region**; DPA/transfer review is a production gate. Identity/session export and password migration later are harder; use an adapter at the auth-to-domain boundary. **Localization and mockup cost:** Hobby's prebuilt UI can be localized (Romanian and English are available), but Clerk calls that UI localization [experimental](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/customizing-clerk/localization), and it does **not** translate outgoing email. Approved mockups and the current visible `DEBIRO` brand casing remain binding; use Clerk's custom auth flows where prebuilt components would visibly depart from them or show unapproved vendor branding. That is UI integration work, not a reason to redesign screens. Hobby excludes custom Clerk email templates. The selected integration therefore owns localized verification/recovery email copy and delivery through Resend: disable Clerk delivery per required template and consume Clerk's signed `email.created` webhook with the verification data, choose the recipient's `ro`/`en` locale (including before a local user exists), send idempotently, retry failures, and never log codes/links. [Clerk delivery guidance](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/development/troubleshooting/email-deliverability) documents that route; it is additional integration and email-deliverability responsibility, not a free built-in Clerk feature. Count those messages inside the 1,000/month and 100/day Resend assumptions. Test this exact flow on Hobby before treating the cost and localization promise as production-ready. Pro email-template access alone does not prove per-recipient bilingual delivery.

Neon's managed Better Auth is a relevant emerging third option because it keeps identity in the selected PostgreSQL and removes self-hosted auth operations, but it is [not a drop-in replacement](https://neon.com/blog/neon-auth-branchable-identity-in-your-database): Neon requires its Auth SDK/API and does not support bring-your-own Better Auth plugins or custom server handlers. A complete Romanian/English verification/recovery email path and needed lifecycle controls were not verified for this review, so it is not promoted to a fourth full-stack candidate or silently assumed equivalent to Clerk.

**Decision:** Switch to **Clerk Hobby for authentication identity and sessions only**, with the localized email integration above. Security, reliability, and reduced solo-developer credential/session operations outweigh its free-plan constraints and migration lock-in at the modeled scale, even though bilingual email is still debiro-owned. This is not a claim that US storage is EU residency or that Clerk authorizes business actions. Production must pass a DPA/transfer review; if that legal gate rejects US identity storage, or the Hobby email flow fails validation, revisit this decision before real users are onboarded. Use server-verified Clerk subject as an external identity key, create/reconcile a local debiro user idempotently, and resolve membership/organization/role solely in PostgreSQL on every protected action. Webhooks may reconcile lifecycle changes, but authorization must not wait for a webhook. Supplier links remain separate scoped capabilities. Do not use Clerk Organizations or its B2B add-on for debiro tenancy.

## Other component review

- **Railway:** Keep the already-paid Hobby account for web, finite worker and cron in Amsterdam. The $5 subscription is a real account baseline, shared with other projects, not a debiro-only free grant. Debiro adds metered CPU/RAM/egress; the shared unused credit (unknown, $0–5) determines incremental invoice headroom. Database co-location is cheaper but transfers operations, as above. [Railway pricing](https://docs.railway.com/pricing).
- **R2:** Keep private EU-jurisdiction Standard buckets and S3-compatible presigned staging/final access. At 500 × 2 MB documents plus backup/staging headroom (~1.5 GB average) and a few thousand monthly operations, it fits the **account-wide** 10 GB, 1 million Class A, 10 million Class B allowances if not already consumed elsewhere; modeled MVP charge $0. Egress is free. Lifecycle and deletion/recovery still require application policy. [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), [EU jurisdiction](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/).
- **Email:** Keep Resend for its focused API, domain authentication and $0 Free tier at 1,000/month **only if** peaks stay ≤100/day. First likely paid upgrade is a >100/day reminder/invitation burst or >3,000/month; Pro is $20/month. Resend stores message content/logs in the US despite EU sending; review its DPA/SCCs and minimize message content before customer mail. [Pricing](https://resend.com/pricing), [data location](https://resend.com/security/gdpr). EU-hosted Brevo is a credible alternative (France/Germany, 300/day free), but its free email carries Brevo branding; an unbranded paid tier starts around $18/month ($9 Starter plus $9 logo removal, or $18 Standard), adding cost/operational surface. Reconsider only if US email storage fails the legal review. [Brevo transactional](https://www.brevo.com/products/transactional-email/), [plans/branding](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans).
- **Observability:** Keep Railway structured/redacted logs, metrics and health checks, Neon metrics, and durable queue failure/age records. No paid APM until error volume or seven-day Railway log retention makes diagnosis inadequate. [Railway logs](https://docs.railway.com/observability/logs).
- **OCR/AI:** Keep Poppler native text before local Tesseract Romanian/English OCR, with scanning and bounded worker resources. There is no separate OCR API fee; CPU/RAM is in the worker row. GPT-5 mini text extraction at 200 uploads plus 20% retry allowance is ~$0.47/month, separately usage-driven, and human confirmation remains mandatory. Real-document AI still requires the existing EU-processing/DPA gate. [Model rate](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5-mini).

## Coherent candidates

| Candidate | Security/reliability and maintenance | MVP and traction cost; portability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — TASK-002 stack: Railway + Neon + self-hosted Better Auth + pg-boss + R2 + Resend | Managed data and EU-owned identity, but full auth patching, session/abuse and incident burden stays in debiro. | Same modeled provider bill as B; no auth SaaS fee. Portable identity, more ongoing engineering. |
| **B — Selected: Railway + Neon + Clerk identity + pg-boss + R2 + Resend** | Managed DB and auth reduce two high-risk operating surfaces; debiro keeps tenant authorization and bilingual auth-email delivery. US identity/email transfer reviews, webhook reliability, and external auth outage dependency remain. | ~$18.7 MVP gross (~$13.7–18.7 above existing Railway baseline); ~$85.8 traction gross. Clerk Hobby $0 at modeled users; provider migration harder. |
| C — Railway + Railway PostgreSQL + Clerk + pg-boss + R2 + Resend | One platform and private app↔DB networking, but unmanaged PostgreSQL security, upgrades, PITR, backup and recovery are now a solo-developer duty; common-platform exposure rises. | Illustratively ~$13–14 MVP gross before unmeasured PITR/WAL and recovery labor; traction database resources may grow. Standard PG remains portable, but cheaper invoice is not equal reliability. |

Neither candidate B nor Neon Launch has a contractual uptime SLA at these plans. Production confidence comes from tested restore, monitoring, and operational response, not plan labels. Candidate B is selected for the best balance of data recovery and managed authentication at the modeled cost. The original willingness to pay approximately €20–30 for meaningful reliability/security gains remains sound: a roughly $18.7 modeled gross bill is **not** evidence to weaken managed PostgreSQL, and actual cost can reach ~$26.5 if Neon remains awake. No paid OCR, APM, Redis or extra worker is justified now; these avoidable services were not in either total.

## Consequences and follow-up

1. Update canonical stack/security/deployment docs to use Clerk for identity only. Preserve ADR-0002 as history with a visible partial-supersession note. No accounts, code, or infrastructure are changed here.
2. Keep the 10-minute finite pg-boss worker; validate no always-on queue timers in the web process, queue maintenance/retry/dead-letter behavior, and actual Neon CU-hours during implementation. If the split cannot be made reliable, use the documented ~$26.5 MVP upper scenario rather than weakening job durability.
3. Before customer launch, resolve Clerk and Resend US transfer/DPA reviews; prove localized `ro`/`en` Clerk verification/recovery emails on Hobby end to end, including webhook authentication, expiry, retries and provider outages; measure email peaks; perform restore drills and tenant isolation tests; and satisfy the OpenAI real-document gate. A privacy rejection or failed auth-email path requires a new decision, not silent processing.
4. TASK-003 — UI Foundation & Canonical Design System remains next. This review does not implement it.
